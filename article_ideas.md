# Article ideas

Appended by the daily scan of `src/content`. Check this file for duplicates before adding.

Core themes in existing writing: AI's effect on engineering work and leadership; documentation and prompting as craft; heroism and organisational resilience; myth and epic as lenses on power, mortality and intelligence.

## 2026-09-26

- **The review bottleneck is a leadership problem** (leadership). Recent data puts AI-assisted PRs at roughly 2.5x larger and waiting about 5x longer for a reviewer. Extends the Faros figures in the human-directed work essay: who owns review capacity, and should PR size be capped before generation, not after?
- **Your AGENTS.md is probably too long** (thought). ETH Zurich and others found LLM-generated context files cut task success by about 3% and raise cost by over 20%. Ties to "AI is eating its own documentation": short, human-written, reviewed context beats generated bulk.
- **Blameless is a leader behaviour, not a template** (leadership). Commentary this year says most teams have postmortem templates but few have the culture. Pairs with the heroic developer essay: the first senior person to publish their own mistake changes what the team believes.
- **Gilgamesh, retold again** (myths). A new verse translation by Simon Armitage arrived in 2026, and machine learning has been stitching tablet fragments together via the Fragmentarium project. Angle: how translation choices change the reading of Gilgamesh's arc, plus the odd fit of AI reassembling the oldest epic.

## 2026-09-27

- **The Copilot backfill freeze** (leadership). Manager spans are creeping up (one 2026 analysis puts the average at 12.1 direct reports, up from 10.9 in 2024) as backfills get frozen on the assumption that AI covers the gap. The code volume goes up but the coaching and integration load doesn't shrink. Angle: AI changes what a team produces, not how many people a manager can actually develop. Pairs with the capability debt section of the AI-assisted engineering essay.
- **Context engineering didn't retire the prompt** (thought). This year's line is that prompt engineering is dead and context is 95% of output quality; one 2026 industry report has 82% of IT and data leaders agreeing prompts alone no longer suffice. A counterpoint to "Prompt precision as a design skill": the context is only useful if someone states precisely what they want from it, and that sentence is still a prompt.
- **Talos had the forge but not the mind** (myths). Talos, the bronze guardian of Crete, keeps turning up in 2026 AI commentary as the "first robot", usually via Adrienne Mayor's _Gods and Robots_. Hephaestus made him, but the Athena essay's point is that making and mētis are different gifts. Angle: the Greeks imagined tireless machines and still kept judgement with a goddess, which is a sharper myth for AI than Pandora.
